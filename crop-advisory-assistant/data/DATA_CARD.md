## 1. Dataset overview

**Purpose.** This dataset supports the development and evaluation of a crop advisory assistant that retrieves agricultural information and generates answers grounded in a document collection.

**Intended users.** Students, developers, agricultural educators, and researchers working on crop-information retrieval and question-answering systems.

**Intended use.** Educational and research use. The assistant should support, not replace, advice from qualified agricultural extension officers or agronomists.

**Out-of-scope use.** Do not rely on this dataset alone to make high-stakes decisions about pesticide application, chemical dosage, food safety, or crop treatment.

## 3. Data collection and processing

The pipeline extracts text from agricultural documents, cleans it, divides it into passages, and generates question-answer examples grounded in those passages.

Agricultural documents were processed using the project's data preparation pipeline (`src/data_prep.py`). PDF text was extracted using the `pypdf` library, followed by text cleaning, passage chunking, and filtering. Passages were approximately 150 words long, with a one-sentence overlap between consecutive passages to preserve context. Duplicate content and passages without relevant crop or disease information were removed. PDF extraction may produce incomplete or incorrectly ordered text when documents contain tables, multi-column layouts, headers, footers, unusual formatting, or scanned pages. The pipeline does not perform OCR, so scanned PDFs without an extractable text layer may yield little or no usable text. Extraction quality should therefore be checked against the original documents, particularly where tables or technical agricultural recommendations are involved.

The pipeline generated approximately three question-answer pairs per source passage and applied automated quality filters, including checks for answer length, banned phrases, numerical grounding, lexical overlap, and duplicate examples. The manually reviewed sample is limited and should not be interpreted as proof that all retained answers are correct. Retrieval performance is evaluated using a BM25 ranking method, which ranks source passages according to their relevance to each question. Recall@3 measures the proportion of evaluation questions for which at least one relevant passage appears among the top three retrieved results. Mean Reciprocal Rank at 10 (MRR@10) measures the average reciprocal rank of the first relevant passage, considering only the top ten results; higher values indicate that relevant passages tend to appear closer to the top of the ranking. These metrics evaluate information retrieval, not the factual accuracy, completeness, or agronomic safety of generated answers. The reported scores should be treated as preliminary until the data splits and evaluation statistics have been regenerated using the manually reviewed QA dataset (`data/interim/qa_reviewed.jsonl`). Final results should be reported only after confirming that the evaluation was run on the updated held-out test set and that source-document separation was maintained between training, validation, and test data.

## 7. Language and geographic coverage

The dataset is primarily English-language agricultural information. Its relevance to Rwanda and other East African farming contexts depends on the sources represented in the collection.

The dataset covers beans, maize, cassava, and potatoes, with agricultural information relating to crop diseases, symptoms, factors that favour disease development, and management practices. The source collection focuses on agricultural information relevant to tropical and East African contexts; however, the extent to which individual documents specifically represent Rwanda has not been fully established. The dataset does not claim comprehensive coverage of all crop diseases, geographic regions, or farming systems. Its intended audiences include students, software developers, agricultural educators, researchers, and people developing crop advisory tools. Farmers may use the resulting assistant to explore general agricultural information, but they should consult qualified agricultural extension officers or agronomists before making consequential crop-management decisions. Further validation with Rwandan farmers and local agricultural experts is needed to establish its suitability for specific local conditions.


## 8. Risks and responsible use

- Generated answers may be incorrect, incomplete, or unsupported.
- Source documents may differ in date, authority, geographic relevance, and recommended practices.
- Agricultural recommendations can depend on crop variety, location, season, soil, pest pressure, and local regulations.
- The dataset is not a substitute for expert assessment or field validation.
- Users should consult qualified local agricultural experts for consequential decisions.

## 10. Summary

This dataset is a development resource for a document-grounded crop advisory assistant. Its usefulness depends on source quality, appropriate train/validation/test separation, transparent reporting of synthetic data, and continued human and agricultural-expert evaluation. Counts and metrics in this document should be regenerated whenever the underlying data or pipeline changes.
